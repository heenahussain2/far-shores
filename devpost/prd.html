Product definition / 03 / Review copy

Planetary Travel Guide

APPROVED

A personal astronomy guide presented as an interplanetary travel agency. Explore, maintain your catalogue, and book a fictional holiday.

Read alongside the canonical Markdown PRD. Open each section to inspect behavior, edge cases, and evidence of completion. The marked defaults and visual interpretation were accepted at review.

Passenger journey

The loop, end to end

Open the cover → Explore destinations → browse or search on the separate catalogue page → read a profile → book → discover the verdict → return. Add and edit entries as you learn; saved changes survive refresh.

Look and feel

A magazine of far shores

Updated direction from your early build references: a Far Shores magazine cover, destination grid, and detailed planet profiles.

Near-black, warm off-white and orange; serif cover title, oversized bold grid/profile headings, orbital linework, and tinted photographic spheres. Decorative imagery is not natural-color science. Styling preserves the eight-planet catalogue and current travel behavior.

Voice

Dry travel advice

A short qualitative survival label and a deadpan sentence grounded in the conditions.

“Survival outlook: suit-dependent. Keep your habitat sealed and your return ticket handy.”

Tone example only. Scientific content will be verified during preparation; no unsupported survival percentages.

01 / Destination catalogue

What you see and do

The home cover shows “Welcome to your planetary travel guide” and “Explore destinations.” The separate catalogue page contains “Choose your shore,” the destination search, planet cards, and an “Add planet” action. A brief fade-and-slide transition respects reduced-motion preferences.

Cards show name, short description, and approximate distance from Earth. The initial set includes all eight planets, including Earth. Selecting a card opens its profile. Added planets join the same catalogue and search.

Proposed search default

Case-insensitive partial-name matches while typing. Empty search shows all entries.

Empty state

“No destinations found” for an unmatched query, with a clear-search action. Entries are retained.

Proof it works

  • Eight cards appear on first use.
  • Search narrows results; clearing restores them.
  • A saved new planet is searchable and opens its own profile.

02 / Planet profile

Reading order

  1. Oversized name and description on the left, circular planet illustration on the right; narrow screens stack text above imagery.
  2. Scientific details: approximate distance from Earth, journey time, and distance from its Sun/host star.
  3. Atmosphere.
  4. What living there would feel like, labelled artistic interpretation.
  5. “Book the package.”

Edit opens the planet's existing values; a return action goes to the catalogue.

Approximate, with context

“Distances and journey times are approximate and can vary due to planetary movement.” Estimates also state an assumed spacecraft speed and their simplified fictional nature.

Incomplete information

Optional missing details show “Unknown.” Proposed default for missing images: a neutral planet placeholder.

Proof it works

  • The selected planet appears in the agreed reading order.
  • Units, movement note, and speed assumption accompany estimates.
  • Factual conditions and artistic interpretation are distinguishable.
  • A minimal custom entry opens without fabricated details.

03 / Add and edit a planet

Required to start

Name and short description. Other details are optional. All information is editable, including imagery, science, living-there text, packing advice, and survival outlook.

Proposed interaction defaults

Explicit Save and Cancel actions. Saving updates the card and profile. Cancelling discards uncommitted edits. Existing entries begin with their current values.

Validation and persistence

Blank or whitespace-only required fields prevent saving, identify the field, and retain the user's input. Saved changes persist in the same browser; no account or synchronization.

Proposed save-error behavior: explain that saving failed, retain input for retry, and do not claim success. Successful save shows brief feedback.

Proof it works

  • Name and description alone save a new card.
  • Missing required input receives a visible explanation.
  • Edited values appear wherever relevant.
  • Refresh retains additions and changes without duplicating initial planets.
  • Cancel preserves previously saved values.

Image editing and consistent treatment of estimates are specified in 4-spec. Develops scope → The POC Boundary and What “Working” Looks Like.

04 / Book and reveal

The payoff

On the profile, “Book the package” reveals “Travel booked!”, approximate journey time, things to bring, and a qualitative survival outlook with deadpan copy. Clearly labelled fictional; no real reservation or payment.

Incomplete trip details

Show a risk icon on the booking button with a text warning. Booking stays available. The reveal says: “Details unknown. Book at your own risk.”

Known details still appear; missing journey, packing, or survival information stays unknown. The icon indicates missing information, not physical safety.

Proposed default: repeat clicks show the same result without duplicate confirmations. Booking need not persist across refresh.

Proof it works

  • A complete destination reveals confirmation and all three trip sections.
  • A minimal entry shows the risk icon before booking and the exact warning afterward.
  • No missing values are invented.
  • Filling missing trip details removes the incomplete-information warning.

Develops scope → The Unique Kernel.

Now

Catalogue, search, profiles, add/edit forms, browser persistence, and fictional booking reveal. The criteria above define the observable finish line.

Planet data persists. Search, booking reveal, and unsaved edits need not persist.

Deferred / excluded

  • Larger supplied catalogue: manual additions suffice now.
  • Accounts and cross-device sync: single-browser personal use.
  • Extra games, stamps, and calculators: the verdict is the payoff.
  • Real reservations, payment, or survival advice: fictional trips.
  • Live orbital tracking or realistic mission simulation: labelled approximations suffice.

What still needs deciding

Review resolved: the marked interaction defaults and visual interpretation were approved with the plan.

During content preparation: verify factual source material and label artistic licence. This PRD establishes no quantitative scientific estimates.
